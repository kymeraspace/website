Moisture Before Shaving: The Number Most Tanneries Don't Measure

A
Alessandro Burlando
November 21, 2026 · 5 min read

The operator flags a thickness difference on the shaved lot. The first suspect is always the machine calibration. Right after that come the blades: maybe they are not cutting clean anymore.

But what if the thickness was correct when the hide came off the shaving machine, and only changed after a while?

That is where moisture comes in, and with it the check that should happen on the batch of hides before shaving, not after. Wet blue that goes into the machine too wet, or with moisture that varies from stack to stack, can measure right at the outfeed and drift once the fibres relax and the water settles. Calibration and blades get the blame. The real cause was on the pallet.

Why Moisture Changes the Cut

Wet blue is a network of collagen fibres holding a lot of water. How much water it holds changes how those fibres react when the blade cylinder meets them.

  • Too wet: the hide is soft and compressible. Fibres get pushed and flattened instead of cut cleanly, and the leather can recover after the machine and measure thicker than the setting.
  • Too dry: the hide is stiff. The blades work harder, heat builds up, the flesh side can tear or burnish, and blade wear accelerates.
  • Uneven within the lot: this is the real killer. You set the machine once for a lot. If moisture varies from stack to stack, thickness varies too, even with a perfect setting and fresh blades.

Every tannery should work within a defined moisture window at the shaving machine. The exact window depends on the article, the final thickness and the machine. What matters more than the exact number is consistency: a lot that sits within ±2 points shaves predictably.

Where the Variation Comes From

A quick note on the number itself. Moisture is expressed on the total wet blue mass: 50% means 50 kg of water in every 100 kg of wet blue. In practice, wet blue swings from roughly 50–55% to 60–65% moisture, a spread of around ten points (source: Leather International).

The usual causes:

  • Storage time: the longer a pallet waits, the more it loses at the edges and on top.
  • Climate: summer and winter in the same warehouse give different wet blue.
  • Sammying settings and felt condition: a worn or clogged sammying felt extracts less water, and it does so gradually, so nobody notices.
  • Purchased wet blue: transport time, transport conditions and differences between production batches at the supplier.

None of these show up on the delivery note. If you don't measure, you are setting the shaving machine blind.

What It Costs You Downstream

The shaving machine is only the first place moisture bites. In the wet-end, chemicals are dosed on the wet weight of the leather, but they only react with the dry part. So the same recipe delivers a different real dose depending on moisture:

  • High moisture → overdosing. Simulation trials with ±10% variation of the reference mass showed a strong effect on colour in full chrome processes.
  • Low moisture → underdosing. Lower grain strength, lower elongation, lower softness, with stronger effects on thinner leathers.

The result is the problem every tanner hates: a lot that doesn't reproduce the last one, with no obvious cause in the drum log (source: Leather International). We wrote about the colour side of this in Color Control in Manufacturing.

How to Measure It on the Floor

The reference method is oven drying: weigh a sample, dry it, weigh it again. It is standardised (ASTM D6658 covers volatile matter in wet blue and wet white) and it is the right tool for the lab. But it is slow and destructive, so nobody runs it on every stack before shaving.

A 5-Step Routine for the Shaving Department

  1. Set a target window per article. Write it on the work order, next to the target thickness.
  2. Measure every stack before it reaches the machine. Five points per stack, use the average function.
  3. Out of range? Don't shave it yet. Let it rest, send it back through the sammying machine, or keep it as a separate batch with its own setting.
  4. Measure thickness after shaving on a few hides per stack, and log it next to the moisture reading.
  5. Review once a week. Compare moisture against thickness deviations and blade consumption. If moisture keeps creeping up, check the sammying felts before anything else.

FAQ

What is the ideal moisture content of wet blue for shaving?

There is no universal number. It depends on the article, the final thickness and the machine. Keeping the whole lot inside a narrow band matters more than hitting one exact value.

How is wet blue moisture measured?

The lab reference is oven drying (ASTM D6658). On the production floor, portable non-destructive meters such as the Humimeter LM6 give a reading in seconds, and they can be checked against oven samples.

Why does thickness change after the hide has been shaved?

If the reading was correct at the machine and drifts later, check moisture before calibration and blades. Hides shaved too wet, or with uneven moisture across the lot, can change once the fibres relax and the water settles.

Before You Change the Blades Again

If thickness at your shaving machine keeps drifting and you have already changed blades and checked the cylinder, measure moisture before anything else. It is the cheapest test in the department.
